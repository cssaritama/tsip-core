Where price stands in time.

The position of today's price against the prior week, month, quarter, half-year and year, and what happened by the end of past periods that stood in the same place. Every figure carries its sample, its uncertainty and a grade.

How every figure is graded

A frequency without a control is not evidence. Each finding states what it is compared with, and its grade follows published rules, version .

Effects, graded A to F

An effect measured against a control or a null, adjusted for the number of questions asked across the whole report (Benjamini–Hochberg).

  • A, strong. q at most 0.05, the most recent 30% of the sample agrees, at least 200 observations.
  • B, moderate. q at most 0.10 and both halves of the sample agree.
  • C, weak. Nominally significant, but it does not survive the multiple-testing correction.
  • D, inconclusive. No reliable difference from the control.
  • F, contradicted. The most recent data points the other way.

Base rates, reliability A to D

How often something happens. There is no null, so precision and stability decide the grade.

  • 95% interval within ±2.5 pp; earlier and recent samples within 3 pp.
  • Within ±4 pp and 6 pp.
  • Within ±7.5 pp and 10 pp.
  • Wider or less stable.
  • Too few observations to grade.
Random-walk timing
In a random walk a period's high and low cluster near its start and end. Bars are reordered within each period to measure what timing looks like by chance.
Geometry-matched levels
A close near the top of a range reaches the top first more often for purely mechanical reasons. Bands of the same width and position isolate what the real levels add.
Same-position analogs
A period in progress is compared only with past periods at the same point and in the same position against their prior range.
Dependence-aware uncertainty
Market days are not independent. Intervals come from a circular block bootstrap over days, weeks, months or longer.

What these statistics can do

  • Describe how price behaved at each horizon and level over years of data.
  • Compare every effect with a control and grade it on data it was not fitted to.
  • Show where the current period stands and how similar periods ended.
  • Re-grade everything every day as new data arrives.

What they cannot do

  • Guarantee any outcome. Past frequencies are not promises.
  • Include costs, slippage or taxes; returns are gross.
  • Tell anyone to buy or sell. There are no signals here.
  • Turn a weak pattern into an edge by repeating it.

Data and provenance

Only derived statistics are published; raw vendor prices are never redistributed. Every dataset is checksummed, checked for completeness and dated.

Pre-registered hypotheses

Statements fixed in advance with a fingerprint and evaluated later on data that did not exist when they were written.
